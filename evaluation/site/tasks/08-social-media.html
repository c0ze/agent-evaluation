Local means the indexing and summaries run on your computer. Your PDFs and notes aren't uploaded to us to be processed. If you want the specifics on what, if anything, the app sends over the network, ask and we'll lay it out.

Instagram: "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Honest answer: we haven't published anything about Japanese-language support, so I don't want to guess. The 14-day trial is the safest test. If you try it, we'd really like to know how it goes.

Threadmark launch day

LinkedIn post

Threadmark is available today on Windows and macOS.

It is a small desktop app for a familiar research mess: a folder of PDFs and notes you can no longer search by memory. Threadmark turns that folder into a local, searchable evidence map. PDFs and notes from the same folder share it. The launch feature is source-linked summaries. Click a sentence and the app opens the supporting passage, so the paraphrase stays tied to the page it came from.

Processing stays on your computer. The price is $12 a month after a 14-day trial.

Linux remains an investigation. Today's versions are Windows and macOS, and no release date is attached to Linux.

The click is the part worth testing. During the trial, open one real project folder you already know well, so a wrong link is easy to spot.

Replies

LinkedIn: "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

A fair suspicion. Threadmark builds a local evidence map from your PDFs and notes. The launch feature is narrower than a chat window: summary sentences that open the supporting passage when you click them. Processing stays on your computer. If you wanted a back-and-forth with the documents, this is a different tool. The 14-day trial lets you check that on your own files.

LinkedIn: "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

People at a university can try the app on their own computers. We don't sell lab seats, a campus license, or a bundled invoice. The published offer is $12 a month after a 14-day trial, on Windows and macOS. Any reimbursement is between the researcher and their department. I won't add a procurement path.

Bluesky: "no linux, no deal"

Windows and macOS shipped today. Linux is under investigation, and no release date comes with that. If Linux is a requirement, skipping is a fair call.

Bluesky: "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

Local means processing stays on your computer. We don't upload your papers to train a model. I won't add a privacy certification we don't have.

Instagram: "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

Two hours stuck on indexing, after you paid, is a stall, and I'm sorry it's sitting there. Don't post your email, receipt, folder path, or file names. Leave the current folder as it is. Quit Threadmark fully, reopen it, and try a separate small folder of a few PDFs. Reply with only two things: Windows or macOS, and whether that smaller run moves. If it stays frozen too, send that limited note in a private message and stop. Leave payment details and the library out of it.

Instagram: "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

I can't promise that. We haven't published language coverage, so a Japanese literature review stays an open question. Threadmark uses the text already in your local PDFs and notes, then links each summary sentence to a passage. Put a small sample folder through the 14-day trial and judge those links before you pay.
