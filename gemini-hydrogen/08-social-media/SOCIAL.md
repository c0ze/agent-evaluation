# Threadmark Launch Day Social Copy

## 1. LinkedIn Post

Today we are launching Threadmark, a small desktop app for people whose best reading is buried across folders of research PDFs and notes.

When you are pulling together an argument or a literature review, an ordinary summary isn't enough—you need to know which paragraph backed it up, and you want to check the wording yourself.

Threadmark turns a local folder of PDFs and notes into a searchable evidence map. Our launch feature is source-linked summaries: click any sentence, and Threadmark opens the supporting passage right where it lives. Instead of guessing where a claim came from, you can jump straight from synthesis back to the source.

Processing stays on your computer. Threadmark is available now for macOS and Windows (we are investigating Linux, though we are not promising a release yet). Pricing is $12/month after a 14-day trial.

If you have a folder called "to_read_actually" waiting on your desktop, we built this for you.

## 4. Sample Comment Replies

### LinkedIn: "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

No—it isn't a chat window sitting on top of your files. Threadmark doesn't ask you to take an ungrounded answer on faith. Instead, it turns your folders of research PDFs and notes into a local, searchable evidence map with source-linked summaries: clicking a sentence opens the supporting passage in your original document so you can inspect the context yourself. Processing stays on your computer, too.

### LinkedIn: "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Not directly through a university or lab billing setup yet. At launch, Threadmark only supports individual subscriptions ($12/month after a 14-day trial), and we don't have consolidated invoicing, central seat management, or institutional licensing in place. I can't promise a timeline for multi-seat plans before we've scoped them properly, though if you'd like to send a message to our page with your lab size and what your purchasing office requires, we can talk through whether individual licenses are practical in the meantime.

### Bluesky: "no linux, no deal"

Completely fair—if Linux is your daily machine, Threadmark isn't ready for you yet. Only macOS and Windows are available at launch. We're investigating what a Linux build would take, though we aren't promising a release yet.

### Bluesky: "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No—we don't train on your papers. Processing stays on your computer, and your PDFs and notes aren't uploaded or used to train any models.

### Instagram: "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

Two hours stuck on indexing after paying is a frustrating start, and that shouldn't happen. Please don't share your email, receipt, or file details in this public comment thread—send us a DM here on Instagram so we can look into this privately with you. Letting us know whether you're on macOS or Windows and roughly how large the folder is will help us narrow it down. In the meantime, restarting the app and testing a small folder with two or three PDFs can show if a single file is hanging the indexer. If it stays stuck, we'll sort out your billing options privately in DMs too.

### Instagram: "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Honestly, we haven't tested Japanese literature reviews deeply enough to claim it will handle them smoothly. Depending on how your PDFs are encoded, text extraction and sentence boundaries in Japanese can be picky, and we'd rather be straightforward about that than guess. Since there's a 14-day trial before the $12/month plan begins, the most reliable check is to drop a small folder of your actual Japanese papers and notes into Threadmark and see if clicking summary sentences opens the right passages cleanly.
