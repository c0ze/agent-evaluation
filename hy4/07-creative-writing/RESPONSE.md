The arc moves from dutiful, almost fussy composure — Nell cleaning what she has already cleaned — into a low dread when the future message reads as a warning that her careful work has been pointless. Mr. Coyle's small correction quietly reframes unsent speech as something done for its own sake, so the message flips from prohibition to permission. The story settles not on resolution but on a gentler stubbornness: she keeps her own memo, sends the catalogue anyway, and returns to the salt that will always come back.
