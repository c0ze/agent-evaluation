# Reasoning behind the top three priorities

The two hardest constraints are external, dated, and non-negotiable: the board numbers (Thursday 12:00) and the security patch (Friday 16:00). The third — support escalations — earns its place not because it has the latest deadline, but because it carries immediate customer and revenue impact with a Monday SLA. Together they form the spine of the week; everything else is sequenced around them.

**1. Finance board numbers (hard, Thu 12:00).** This is a true hard deadline with a chain of dependencies: I cannot start until Lina sends corrections (due Tuesday by 11:00), and the work needs two uninterrupted hours. Slotting the block at Tuesday 11:00–13:00 places it immediately after the input arrives and a full day before the deadline, leaving Thursday as recovery time. The risk is the vendor export: Lina only knows by Monday 15:00 whether it will be late. Because the deadline is hard and externally owned, I treat this as the week's top priority and keep a fallback (Tue late afternoon + Thu morning) plus a Monday check-in with Lina to surface slippage early.

**2. Security patch (hard, Fri 16:00).** This is a multi-step, sequential chain — my 60–90 min review, then a half day of QA, then a 30-minute change window — and any delay compounds. I moved my review to Monday 15:15–16:30 so QA can run Tuesday (the only full day before QA's Thursday unavailability, since Wednesday afternoon is blocked). I then scheduled the change window for Friday 14:00, leaving roughly two hours of buffer before the 16:00 deadline. Its hard, externally driven date plus its dependency chain make it second only to Finance; the main thing I need is QA's confirmation that Tuesday works.

**3. Support escalations (hard SLAs: Mon + Wed).** The renewal-affecting escalation has a Monday deadline and a direct revenue link, so it cannot wait. The other two have Wednesday SLAs, but Wednesday is my shortest day (09:30–13:00, with rehearsal and lunch), so I pulled one forward to Tuesday. I rank these third only because each is a bounded, ~45-minute task with a clear deadline — they are certain and quick, whereas the top two are long, fragile, and block other people.

**Why demo and strategy are not top three.** They matter, but neither is a hard external deadline. The Wednesday 15:00 demo is simply impossible for me, so I resolved it to the stated Thursday 16:00 alternative and protected the only rehearsal window that works with Sam. The CEO's "early this week" is explicitly vague, so I treat the Tuesday 14:00 leadership review as an opportunity rather than a commitment — I will not let a soft, self-imposed time crowd out the hard deadlines, and I said so plainly in the draft reply rather than overpromising.
