# Threadmark launch day: social copy

## 1. LinkedIn post

Threadmark is out today for Windows and macOS.

It's a small desktop app for people whose work lives in folders of research PDFs and notes. Point it at a folder and it builds a local, searchable evidence map of what's inside.

The feature we're launching with is source-linked summaries. Each sentence in a summary is tied to the passage that supports it: click the sentence and that passage opens. Our view is simple. A summary should be easy to check, not something you have to take on faith.

Processing stays on your computer. Your PDFs and notes aren't uploaded to be indexed or summarised.

Pricing is $12/month after a 14-day trial. We're investigating Linux, but we aren't promising it or putting a date on it.

If you run literature reviews, draft grant applications, or keep a "read later" folder that has quietly turned into a library, we built Threadmark for that particular kind of mess.

## 4. Replies to sample comments

### LinkedIn: "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Fair thing to ask, given how many of those there are. Threadmark isn't a chat window. It's a desktop app that indexes a folder of PDFs and notes into a searchable evidence map, on your own computer. The summaries are the part we care most about: every sentence links to the passage it came from, and clicking it opens that passage. So instead of asking you to trust an answer, it shows you where to check it. If a summary sentence doesn't hold up against its source, that's easy to see, and we'd like to hear about it.

### LinkedIn: "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Good question, and I'd rather give you a real answer than a guess. The published price today is $12/month after a 14-day trial; we haven't announced a lab or institutional plan. If you send us a direct message with roughly how many people are in the lab and how your university usually handles software purchases (card, invoice, purchase order), we'll tell you plainly what we can and can't do right now.

### Bluesky: "no linux, no deal"

Understood, and that's a fair line to draw. Right now it's Windows and macOS only. We're investigating Linux, but I won't promise it or give a date we can't stand behind. If it changes, we'll say so here.

### Bluesky: "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

Local means the indexing and summaries run on your computer. Your PDFs and notes aren't uploaded to us to be processed. If you want the specifics on what, if anything, the app sends over the network, ask and we'll lay it out.

### Instagram: "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

I'm sorry. Two hours stuck after paying is not okay, and we want to fix it with you. Please send us a DM (not here, so your account details stay private) with your OS (Windows or macOS), roughly how many files are in the folder, and whether anything on screen has changed since it started. We'll pick it up from there.

### Instagram: "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Honest answer: we haven't published anything about Japanese-language support, so I don't want to guess. The 14-day trial is the safest test. Point Threadmark at a small folder of your Japanese PDFs and check whether search finds what you expect and the summary links land on the right passages. If you try it, we'd really like to know how it goes.
