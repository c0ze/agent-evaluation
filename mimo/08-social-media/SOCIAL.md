# Social launch copy — Threadmark

## LinkedIn post

Threadmark is out today.

Research tends to arrive as a folder of PDFs and notes, some with names like final_v3_REAL.pdf. Threadmark is a small desktop app that turns that folder into a local, searchable evidence map. Processing stays on your computer. The files aren't sent off to be indexed.

The launch feature is source-linked summaries. Each summary sentence is tied to its source: click the sentence and the supporting passage opens beside it. Handy when a draft claim needs checking before it travels any further, or when you need to remember whether the paper actually said the thing you wrote in your outline last month.

It's $12/month after a 14-day trial, on Windows and macOS. If your week includes "wait, where did I read that?", the trial is the fastest way to see whether the map earns its keep. Happy to answer questions about how it works here.

## Comment replies

### LinkedIn — “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”

Fair question — there's no chat window in Threadmark. You search and browse an evidence map built from your own folder. Summaries are source-linked: click a sentence and the supporting passage opens beside it, so you can check the wording against the PDF instead of taking a chatbot's word for it.

### LinkedIn — “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”

Not at launch — it's individual subscriptions only ($12/month after the 14-day trial). We don't have a lab or multi-seat plan, and I won't invent a timeline for one. If a lab wants to evaluate it, each person can run the trial on their own machine. Group-purchase questions are welcome; I'll answer with what's actually possible today.

### Bluesky — “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”

no. indexing and summaries run on your computer, and your papers aren't sent to us for processing. that's what local means here — not a softer marketing version of the word.
