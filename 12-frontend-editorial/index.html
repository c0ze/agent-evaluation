Cover story

The River Keeps Its Receipts

By Imani Vale · Field essay · 18 pages

Along a drought-exposed riverbed, shop ledgers, bottle caps, and vanished street plans surface together. A field essay about civic memory and what infrastructure tries to forget.

Vale walks the bed at first light with former shopkeepers and a retired surveyor, reading the exposed ground the way an archivist reads a box that was never meant to be opened. Every object is logged, photographed, and — where its owner can be found — returned. What remains is an argument: a city remembers most honestly at its lowest level.

“The river does not erase. It files things where only a dry year can find them.”

Night Bus Atlas

Tomás Iriarte

The unofficial geography drawn by cleaners, nurses, bakers, and drivers while a city’s daytime map is asleep.

Iriarte rides the last and first buses for a month, collecting hand-drawn route corrections from the people who actually depend on them. The result is printed as a working atlas — tear it out and use it.
