# Threadmark Launch Day Social Copy

## LinkedIn Post

Most research folders don't fail from a lack of reading. They fail three weeks later, when you remember a claim you want to cite and can't remember which PDF, appendix, or margin note it came from.

Today we're launching Threadmark, a small desktop app that turns folders of research PDFs and notes into a local, searchable evidence map.

Our launch feature is source-linked summaries. When you read a summary sentence, clicking it opens the supporting passage directly in the underlying document, so you can verify wording and context instead of taking a paraphrase on faith.

All processing stays on your computer—your library doesn't leave your machine.

Threadmark is available now for macOS and Windows at $12/month after a 14-day trial. We're investigating a Linux version, though we aren't promising a release yet.

If your literature review currently lives across nested folders and half-remembered filenames, Threadmark is ready to try.

## Sample Comment Replies

### LinkedIn: “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”

No—Threadmark isn't a chat window over your files. It builds a local, searchable evidence map from your folders of PDFs and notes. Our launch feature is source-linked summaries: click any sentence and Threadmark opens the supporting passage in the original document. The focus is letting you check a claim directly against the source text, rather than taking a conversational answer on faith, while keeping all processing on your own computer.

### LinkedIn: “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”

Right now, Threadmark only supports individual subscriptions ($12/month after a 14-day trial)—we don't have centralized lab billing, multi-seat licensing, or institutional invoicing at launch. If your group wants to try it today, individual seats are the only option available. You're welcome to message us here with what your university's procurement process would require; we're noting those details, though we aren't promising institutional plans on a set timeline.

### Bluesky: “no linux, no deal”

Fair enough—completely respect sticking to your OS. We launched on macOS and Windows first; we're investigating Linux, though we aren't promising a build yet. No hard feelings if you wait to see a real package.

### Bluesky: “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”

No. Local means processing happens on your computer—your PDFs and notes aren't sent to us, and we don't train on your library. Your files stay on your machine.

### Instagram: “Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?”

Honest answer: we haven't tested Japanese literature reviews thoroughly enough to guarantee good results. PDF text extraction and summaries can behave unevenly with Japanese encoding or mixed layouts, and we don't want to overpromise. Because Threadmark includes a 14-day trial before the $12/month plan starts, the best check is trying a small folder of your actual papers and seeing whether the source-linked passages line up cleanly. If you do test it, feel free to DM us how it holds up—no promises on language-specific work yet, though.
