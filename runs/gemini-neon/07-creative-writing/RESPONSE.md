The story moves Gwen from guarded, procedural detachment—using archival order to wall off her grief over both a drowning town and a silent parting with her daughter—into the dread of believing she is doomed to a decade of lonely self-reproach. When the future message shifts from an older self's bleak confession into her grown daughter's hard-won forgiveness, that dread opens into quiet relief and recognition. By the final line, Gwen lets her composure fall away, stepping out of the role of neutral witness to leave an unguarded voice of her own in the archive.
