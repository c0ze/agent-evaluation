The story opens in dread: Sera hears her future voice as a polite verdict and as a refusal of rescue from a room the sea has already entered. Recognition turns that dread to shame when the same words become the answer she still owes Ellis, unsent even ten years later. Filing those unfinished sentences with the town's, and shutting the accidental laugh back in the drawer, brings a restrained release. The feeling that remains is the quieter relief of a distinction made by hand while the salt continues its work.
