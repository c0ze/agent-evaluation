The arc moves from endurance to dread to inheritance. Ilse begins as someone dutifully holding other people's unsaid words, treating the archive as a burden that is quietly killing her building, and the future message first arrives as a haunting — evidence of a drowning future that punishes her for staying. On the second pass she hears it as what it is: a report, not a warning, delivered by a successor who learned her voice from her own tapes. The emotional turn is that her labor was never solitary or temporary — the last line she writes is a fact worth telling somebody, and she is that somebody — so the story closes not on rescue but on the decision to keep working and to leave the box for whoever is next. The salt carries that reversal physically: the thing that was destroying her equipment turns out to be the only thing sealing the seams and keeping the work alive.
