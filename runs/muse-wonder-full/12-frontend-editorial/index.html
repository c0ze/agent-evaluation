The River Keeps
Its Receipts

By Imani Vale · Photographs from the dry reach, late August

Along a drought-exposed riverbed, shop ledgers, bottle caps, and vanished street plans surface together.

It is an archive nobody filed. The river kept it the way a pocket keeps receipts — crumpled, out of order, and exact about small sums. A field essay about civic memory and what infrastructure tries to forget: the bridge that replaced a ferry, the market the flood maps redrew, the names that were only ever written on the underside of things.

“The river kept it the way a pocket keeps receipts — crumpled, out of order, and exact about small sums.”

Vale walks the dry reach with the people who still read it: a ledger-keeper’s granddaughter, a diver who now works with a bucket and a brush, a town clerk deciding what, this time, gets written down. Low water, she argues, is not an absence. It is a disclosure.

Margin note — Every object named in this essay was photographed in place and left where it lay. The full inventory is printed as a fold-out at p. 96.
