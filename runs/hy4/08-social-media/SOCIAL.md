# Threadmark — launch day social

## LinkedIn post

Today we're launching Threadmark, a small desktop app for anyone with a folder of research PDFs and notes who can never quite find the passage they remember reading.

Threadmark turns that folder into a local, searchable evidence map. The feature we're most excited about is source-linked summaries: every sentence links back to the exact passage that supports it. Click a sentence and you're looking at the original text, not a paraphrase you have to take on faith.

Everything happens on your own computer. Your files aren't uploaded anywhere and we don't use them to train anything — that's a deliberate choice, and why Threadmark is a desktop app rather than a browser tab.

It's available now for Windows and macOS at $12 a month after a 14-day trial, so you can try it on a real project before deciding. Linux is something we're investigating, but we won't promise a date we can't keep.

If you work with dense source material and want to see where each summary line came from, we'd like you to take a look.

## Replies

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

No. Threadmark doesn't produce free-form answers to chat with; it builds a searchable map of your folder and writes summaries where each sentence links to the passage it came from. The point is that you can check the source, not hold a conversation that might drift away from it. There's no conversational bot in the app — the linking is the feature. If you've been burned by tools that rephrase your documents confidently and loosely, that's the specific thing we built away from.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Not through a formal program right now. Threadmark is sold as a single $12/month subscription per user after a 14-day trial, and we haven't set up lab, team, or institutional licensing. A lab could buy individual subscriptions and expense them, but we don't want to imply there's a multi-seat plan when there isn't one. If centralized billing or a purchase order matters for your group, tell us what you'd need and we'll consider it — though we can't promise a timeline, and we'd rather say so up front than let you plan around something that doesn't exist yet.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Fair. Linux is being investigated but we haven't committed to a build, so we won't pretend one is coming or take your money on a maybe. Windows and macOS are what exist today. If you tell us which distro matters to you, we'll keep it in mind as we look — still no promise.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No — local means the processing stays on your computer and your papers aren't uploaded to us. There's nothing of yours sitting on our servers to train on, and we don't have a training pipeline for customer documents at all. If a summary ever appears to draw on something outside your folder, that's a bug worth reporting rather than a hidden data policy.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

It depends on the files, and we don't want to oversell it. Threadmark indexes and links text it can read in your PDFs and notes, but we haven't validated how well source-linked summaries hold up on Japanese-language material — most of our testing has been on English. If your PDFs have selectable text rather than scanned images, trying a small folder during the 14-day trial is the honest way to find out. We'd rather you test it on your own sources than take our word for it.
