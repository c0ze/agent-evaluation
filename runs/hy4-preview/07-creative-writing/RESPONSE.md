# Note on the intended emotional arc

The story moves Nadia from treating the future message as a survival manual — a set of orders that make her smaller, more cautious, more alone — to recognizing it as a request: a woman ten years out, homesick for a room that no longer exists, asking the only person still standing in it to save forty minutes of sound for her. The turn is carried by a word the salt has eaten on the playback head, so that "don't go down to the water" resolves into "don't go down to the water without the recorder," and every line before it shifts from warning to commission. It ends in acceptance rather than rescue: she cannot save the town, but she can witness it and let herself be loved, and the salt beginning again on the windscreen says the losing and the saving are the same ongoing weather.
