Today we are launching Threadmark, a desktop app designed to help researchers navigate dense folders of papers and reading notes.

Instead of treating documents like a generic prompt box, Threadmark indexes your files into a local, searchable evidence map. ## 4. Comment Replies

### LinkedIn: “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”
No. Threadmark is a desktop application centered on an evidence map rather than an open-ended conversational wrapper. Instead of generating ungrounded answers, our core feature is source-linked summaries: every generated sentence links directly to the specific supporting passage in your local PDF or note. You click a sentence, and the app reveals the exact source text so you can evaluate the evidence yourself. Furthermore, all indexing and processing runs on your own computer without sending your research to external APIs.

### LinkedIn: “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”
Currently, Threadmark is available individually at $12 per month per seat (with a 14-day free trial) on macOS and Windows. We do not have institutional or lab-wide licensing tiers set up yet. If lab members wish to use it today, each researcher can start an individual trial. If your department requires centralized purchasing or consolidated invoicing, feel free to reach out to us at contact@threadmark.app and we can discuss manual billing arrangements for your team.

### Bluesky: “no linux, no deal”
Understood, and that's completely fair. Right now we only run on macOS and Windows. We're actively investigating a Linux build, but we aren't making roadmap promises until we know it runs cleanly and reliably. We'll announce it here if and when that happens.

### Bluesky: “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”
No, not at all. Local means fully local: all parsing, indexing, and processing runs on your own machine. Your PDFs and notes never leave your computer, nothing is sent to our servers, and we never collect, inspect, or train any models on your files.

### Instagram: “I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours.”
Two hours is definitely not normal, and we're sorry you're running into that right after subscribing. Complex formatting, corrupted PDFs, or large file scans can sometimes cause the local indexer to hang. Please force-quit the application to prevent further lockup. To troubleshoot this safely without sharing account or private document details in a public comment, please send us a direct message here or email support@threadmark.app with your operating system version. If you can open the app's Help menu, selecting "Export Log" will give us the error diagnostics without including your document contents. We'll help get your library indexing properly.

### Instagram: “Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?”
Threadmark is built and tested primarily around English-language texts right now. While the desktop app can store and display UTF-8 text files, Japanese text segmentation and sentence-level source linking haven't been tuned or validated for Japanese literature yet. Because of that, summaries and passage links may not behave reliably. We’d recommend testing your specific Japanese PDFs during the 14-day free trial before paying to see whether the current parser handles your source material adequately.
