The story moves Ines from dread, hearing what seems to be her older self telling her a decade of preservation was wasted, to the quieter grief of recognizing her dead mother's voice and an unsent message of forgiveness she arrived too late to receive. What first sounds like a verdict becomes permission, and the ending lets her take up her mother's place at the wall, speaking to her in a voice they share, without saying outright whether she will finally let the town go.
