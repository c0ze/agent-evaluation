# Threadmark — launch day social copy

## LinkedIn post

Most research tools ask you to trust a summary. Threadmark shows you where it came from.

Threadmark is a small desktop app for people who accumulate folders of PDFs and notes, then cannot find the one sentence they half-remember. It turns those folders into a local, searchable evidence map.

The launch feature is source-linked summaries: click any sentence and the supporting passage opens beside it. You can check the claim instead of taking it on faith.

Processing runs on your computer. Your papers are not uploaded for indexing.

It costs $12/month after a 14-day trial, available now on Windows and macOS. Linux is being investigated, and we would rather say that plainly than imply a date.

If you have ever spent an afternoon hunting for a citation you know exists, this was built for that afternoon.

## Replies

**LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"**

Fair question. There is no chat box, and the summaries are not the product — the links are. Threadmark's job is to point at the exact passage behind a sentence so you can read it yourself and disagree with the summary. It is a reading tool, not an answer machine.

**LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"**

Not yet. Today it is an individual subscription; there is no institutional billing or seat management. I do not want to promise a lab plan before one exists. If you tell me roughly how many people and how your procurement works, I will keep it in mind and can follow up when there is something concrete.

**Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"**

Understood. Linux is not available, and I will not pretend a date exists. It is being investigated. Windows and macOS are the honest answer today.

**Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"**

No. Indexing and summaries run on your machine. Your papers are not uploaded to us to build the map, so there is no corpus on our side to train on.

**Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."**

That is a real failure, not just a slow first run, and I am sorry it happened on launch day. Please DM us the app version, your operating system, and whether the progress indicator is still moving. We will work through the indexing log with you there. Please do not post file paths or your account email in the comments — we will take those privately.

**Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"**

Honest answer: I cannot confirm it yet. Threadmark depends on the text already embedded in your PDFs, and Japanese search and summaries have not been validated. If your sources are scanned images, that is a separate problem. I would rather say so than have you pay for a trial that disappoints.
