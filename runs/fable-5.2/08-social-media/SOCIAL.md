blockquotes are for the person posting.

## 4. Replies

### LinkedIn: "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

> Reasonable suspicion, there are a lot of those. What Threadmark does is build a searchable map of your folder and write summaries in which each sentence links to the passage it came from. Click the sentence, read the source. If the passage doesn't support the sentence, you see that straight away instead of finding out later. It also runs on your computer rather than in a browser tab. Whether that's different enough for your work is a fair thing to judge in the 14-day trial.

### LinkedIn: "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

> Honest answer: the only pricing we have today is $12/month after a 14-day trial. We don't have a lab or site plan to announce, and we'd rather not make one up in a comment thread. If you message us with roughly how many people are in the lab and how your university prefers to buy software, we'll tell you plainly what we can and can't do.

### Bluesky: "no linux, no deal"

> Fair. It's Windows and macOS today. We're investigating Linux, and we won't promise it until we know we can ship it properly.

### Bluesky: "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

> No. Local means the processing happens on your computer. Your papers aren't sent to us for that, so we don't have them to train on.

### Instagram: "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

> Two hours on an indexing screen right after paying is not how this should go, and we're sorry. Please send us a DM so we can look into it properly. It helps to know whether you're on Windows or macOS, which app version you have, and roughly how many files are in the folder. Please don't post your email or payment details here. We'll handle the account side in the DM, and we can talk about the charge there too.

### Instagram: "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

> We don't want to hand you a confident yes we can't back up. The 14-day trial is the safest way to find out: point Threadmark at a handful of your Japanese PDFs and click a few summary sentences to see whether they open the right passage. If they don't, tell us. That's exactly the kind of report we want to hear.

## Check before posting

The brief leaves four things open. The copy is written so that none of them is
stated as fact, but each is worth a look.

- **Training reply.** "Your papers aren't sent to us" is inferred from
  "processing stays on the user's computer". Confirm it matches the privacy
  policy, including any telemetry or crash reports.
- **Outage reply.** The brief gives no support address, so the reply routes to
  Instagram DMs. Swap in the support channel if there is one. It mentions the
  charge without promising a refund, because no refund policy was given.
- **Lab seats.** No volume or institutional pricing is in the brief, so the
  reply says there is none to announce and takes the conversation private.
- **Japanese.** Language support isn't in the brief, so the reply declines to
  say yes or no. Replace it with a direct answer if the team has one.
