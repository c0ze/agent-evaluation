# Threadmark — launch day social

## 1. LinkedIn post (120–180 words)

Today we're launching Threadmark, a small desktop app for people who live in
piles of research PDFs and notes.

The problem is familiar: you read a lot, highlight a lot, and still can't find
the one passage that backs a claim when you need it. Threadmark turns those
folders into a searchable evidence map.

The launch feature is source-linked summaries. When it summarizes, every
sentence stays tied to the passage it came from. Click a sentence and the
original text opens beside it, so you're never asked to take the summary's word
for anything.

It all runs on your own computer. Your PDFs aren't uploaded for processing.

Threadmark is available now for Windows and macOS, at $12/month after a 14-day
trial — enough time to point it at a real folder before you decide.

We'd rather show you the one thing it does well than oversell it. If you work
in the literature, try it and tell us where it falls short.

## 4. Replies to the six sample comments

### LinkedIn — “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”

Fair question. The difference is the linking. Threadmark isn't a chat window;
it builds an evidence map where each summarized sentence stays attached to the
passage it came from. Click a sentence and the original text opens beside it,
so you're never asked to trust a paraphrase on faith. It also runs locally on
your machine — your PDFs aren't shipped to a server to be processed. A chatbot
gives you answers; Threadmark keeps the receipts.

### LinkedIn — “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”

I'd like to give you a clean yes, but we don't have a published lab or
volume-license plan yet, and I'd rather not invent one here. If you message us
directly with roughly how many seats you have in mind, I'll get you a real
answer instead of a guess. Site purchasing just isn't something we've announced
yet.

### Bluesky — “no linux, no deal”

fair. Windows + macOS are out now. Linux is being investigated, but I won't
promise it and there's no timeline — I'd rather tell you straight than string
you along. If it's a dealbreaker, genuinely no hard feelings.

### Bluesky — “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”

No. Local means your PDFs are processed on your own machine — they aren't
uploaded to us, and nothing you add is used to train anything. If that ever
changed, it'd be something we'd say out loud, not bury in the terms.

### Instagram — “I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours.”

That's genuinely frustrating, especially after paying — I'm sorry. I don't want
to troubleshoot your account out in the open, so please send us a DM (or use
the in-app support link) and we'll pick it up there. If you can, include your
OS, the Threadmark version, and roughly how many PDFs are in the folder; that
helps us find the stuck step faster. Nothing account-specific needs to be
posted here.

### Instagram — “Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?”

Honestly, I don't want to promise that, because I haven't tested Japanese PDFs
enough to stand behind it. The source-linking works on whatever text we can
parse, and non-Latin scripts can be hit or miss. The trial is here for exactly
this: point it at a couple of real Japanese papers and tell me what holds up
and what breaks. I'd rather learn from your stack than guess.
