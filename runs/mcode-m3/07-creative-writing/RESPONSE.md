The story traces Mara from detached professional routine through quiet bewilderment into a small secular tenderness: she begins by hearing the future message as a prohibition against her own life's work, and ends by hearing it as an inheritance — a request that one unsaid thing be carried forward, untranscribed, by whoever outlives the room it was made in. The arc moves from an archival ethic of total preservation toward a gentler one of selective remembrance, where some things belong not in databases but in the small parcels of those still here. Salt on the electronics threads the two halves together: a slow crystallization that is at once ruin and a way of keeping, happening to the recorders and the people at the same unhurried rate.
