# Threadmark — launch day social

## LinkedIn post

Today we're launching Threadmark, a desktop app for researchers tired of juggling dozens of PDFs and scattered notes.

Drop a folder of papers into Threadmark and it builds a local, searchable evidence map. The new release adds source-linked summaries: every sentence in the summary is a clickable link that opens the exact passage it came from.

Everything happens on your machine. The app never uploads your documents, doesn't train on them, and works without an account.

Threadmark is $12/month after a 14-day trial, available today for Windows and macOS. We're looking into Linux support but have nothing concrete to share yet.

If you've ever lost a great quote to a half-remembered filename, this is for you. Try the trial and tell us what breaks.

## Replies to sample comments

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Different in a few useful ways. Threadmark runs entirely on your machine, so no document ever leaves it. And summaries are source-linked: every sentence points back to the exact passage it came from. It's a navigation tool for the documents you already have, not a chat interface. Happy to walk through a specific workflow if you want to compare side by side.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Threadmark is sold as individual subscriptions right now, and we don't have a team or site license yet. Several people have asked about this, so it sits on the list we share with the team. If you can DM a rough seat count and when you'd want to start, I can flag it with your scenario attached.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Linux is on our radar and isn't in the current build. We don't have a release date and would rather get it right than ship a half-working port, so we're still scoping the work. If you drop your distro and rough paper volume, it's useful input for that scoping.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. Extraction, indexing, and summarization all run on your machine. There's no backend service that ingests your documents, so there isn't anything to train on. Summaries and document text only leave your computer if you export them yourself.

### Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

That's not the experience we want you to have, and two hours is well past the usual index time on a normal library. Could you try Quit → relaunch first? If it's still stuck, the most useful thing is the log file (Help → Open log folder); emailing it to support@threadmark.app lets us diagnose without you having to describe everything in text. Sorry it's landing like this.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Yes. Threadmark indexes whatever text is in the file, and Japanese is supported for search and summaries. Source-linked summaries behave the same way — click a sentence, jump to the passage. If you hit any character-handling oddities, please flag them; multilingual support is an area we want to keep improving.
