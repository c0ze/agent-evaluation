# Reasoning behind the top three priorities

## How I ranked them

I used four tests, in this order:

1. **Is the date external and hard?** A missed board pack or a missed patch deploy is
   a fact about the world that I cannot negotiate away on Friday afternoon. A missed
   "early this week" is a conversation.
2. **How long and how brittle is the chain?** Long chains break early and break
   silently. The patch chain runs through a second team (QA) who has exactly one
   usable day.
3. **Is the input outside my control?** Lina's corrections depend on a vendor export.
   That uncertainty has to be bought with calendar space, not with optimism.
4. **What is the cost of being wrong?** Security exposure and circulated wrong
   numbers are both expensive and visible; a one-day delay on a demo is not.

Applying those tests drops the CEO strategy outline and the customer demo out of the
top three — not because they are unimportant (the outline is the highest-visibility
item and the demo is customer-facing), but because **both have soft dates and both
have already been given protected focus time that does not compete with the hard
chain**. Priorities are what I protect when something breaks, and those two are the
things I am willing to break.

---

## 1. Security patch deployed by Friday 16:00

This is first because it is the only item where **the deadline is hard and the chain
is long and single-threaded**: my review (60–90 min) → QA (half a day) → 30-minute
change window → verification, all inside a Friday 16:00 close. QA is unavailable
Thursday, so there is exactly one safe leg (Wednesday) and one fallback leg (Friday
morning) that leaves no room for a second slip. Everything else in the week can be
re-sequenced; this cannot.

The practical consequence is the week's least intuitive move: **the review is pulled
forward to Monday afternoon**, ahead of work that feels more urgent. It is the
cheapest possible insurance — 90 minutes on Monday protects a Friday deadline that
would otherwise depend on a Tuesday that is already carrying the board numbers and
the CEO draft.

## 2. Board numbers final by Thursday 12:00

Second because the date is genuinely hard and the consequence is not quietly
recoverable — once the pack circulates with the numbers in it, the error is public
inside the company. But it ranks below the patch for one specific reason: **the chain
is short and fully mine**. Once Lina's corrections land, it is two hours of work. Two
uninterrupted hours exist on Tuesday 11:00–13:00, and there is a legal fallback on
Thursday 10:00–12:00. A short chain with a fallback is less dangerous than a long
chain without one.

The risk here is entirely the **vendor export**, so the real priority is not the two
hours — it is the **Monday 15:00 checkpoint with Lina**. That single conversation
decides whether Tuesday works or the Thursday hold is needed, and it is the only
monitoring point in the week that can move a hard deadline.

## 3. The renewals escalation response, Monday

Third, and deliberately so. It is 45 minutes, it is near-term revenue, and it is the
only item in the inbox with a stated same-day commitment attached to money. It ranks
above the other two escalations because those have Wednesday SLAs that Tuesday can
absorb comfortably, but below the two hard deadlines because missing it costs an
unhappy account, not a failed obligation.

It is placed at **Monday 10:30, immediately after the recurring meeting** — late
enough that triage is done, early enough that the response does not slip into
Tuesday, where it would compete with the board numbers.

---

## The trade-off I am naming out loud

The CEO strategy outline is three focused hours with no deadline attached. I am
spending those hours on Monday, which means it is *scheduled*, not *prioritised*. If
the patch review overruns or Lina's corrections are late, **the outline revision
block on Friday is what gets dropped first**, and I will say so early rather than
quietly shipping a thin draft on Friday evening. The one thing I will not trade away
is lunch, the Wednesday afternoon block, or the Friday 16:00 deploy.
