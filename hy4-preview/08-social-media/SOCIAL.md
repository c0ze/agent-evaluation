# Threadmark — launch day social

## 1. LinkedIn post

Threadmark is out today.

It began with a problem I kept running into: a folder of a few hundred PDFs, a
chapter half written, and one sentence I remembered reading but could not find
again. Searching filenames does not help when what you remember is an idea.

Threadmark reads the folder on your own computer and builds a searchable evidence
map. Every summary line is linked to its source — click a sentence and the
supporting passage opens in context, with the file it came from. If a statement
cannot be traced to a passage, it does not appear.

Processing stays local. Your papers are not uploaded, and they are not used to
train anything.

It is a small desktop app, not a service to tend. Windows and
macOS are available now. Linux is the request we hear most; we are investigating
it, but we are not putting a date on it until we can keep one.

$12/month after a 14-day trial. If your work depends on showing where a claim
came from, that is who this is for.

## 4. Replies to sample comments

**LinkedIn: “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”**

Fair question, and the honest answer is: there is no chat. You do not ask it
anything. Threadmark indexes the folder on disk and produces summary lines, each
one tied to a specific passage you can open and read. If a sentence cannot be
traced back to a passage, it does not get shown — that constraint is the actual
product, not a feature bolted onto a language model. If it helps, I am happy to
run it on a folder of yours and show you the output rather than describe it.

**LinkedIn: “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”**

Yes, but not through a self-serve form today — multi-seat arrangements are
handled directly. Send us a note with the lab size and whether you need a single
lab or something campus-wide, and we will tell you what we can do and what it
costs. I would rather scope it properly with you than promise a procurement path
we have not built yet.

**Bluesky: “no linux, no deal”**

completely fair. linux is the thing we get asked about most and we're looking
into it — i'd rather say that than hand you a date we might miss. nothing to buy
today then; the 14-day trial is there if you ever want to try it on another os.

**Bluesky: “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”**

no. the work happens on your machine and the files don't leave it — we never have
them, so there's nothing to train on. that's also why there's an indexing step
instead of an instant answer: it's your computer doing the reading.

**Instagram: “I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours.”**

Two hours is not right, and I am sorry — that is our problem, not your folder's.
Please DM us so we can look at it with you; I would rather not go through account

**Instagram: “Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?”**

It should: the index and search work on the text itself, so Japanese sources are
read and linked like any other. It is not something we have tuned carefully yet,
so expect the phrase segmentation to be rougher than it is for English. The trial
runs 14 days — try it on a handful of your own Japanese PDFs before you decide.
