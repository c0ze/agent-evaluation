The intended arc runs from numb competence through dread into release. Vera first hears the future message as a verdict on her life's work — an epitaph for the archive and, implicitly, for her staying — and the reader is meant to share that misreading until the pronoun turns at the end, when "Don't save it" changes from damnation into permission. The closing feeling is a lightened grief: not triumph, but the relief of a truth finally transmitted instead of preserved.
