The story traces Eli’s shift from defensive preservation—hoarding the town’s unsent voices in airtight cases out of fear that letting go means erasure—to a terrified fatalism when he first hears his older voice and mistakes it for a lonely invitation to drown with his failed archive. As he filters the recording and recognizes the acoustic signatures of the high pine bluff and Soren’s presence, that dread transforms into quiet relief and release. By opening the cases and the sluice gate to the bay, he accepts that surrendering the artifacts to the changing world is what allows both the town’s memory and his own life inland to endure.
