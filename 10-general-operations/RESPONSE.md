# Why these are the top three priorities

1. **Monday's renewal escalation comes first because its response is due earliest and it puts a renewal at risk.** The plan allocates the stated 45 minutes on Monday and asks Support for the exact cutoff during morning triage. A response is not a promise of complete resolution; further work gets an owner and next step. The two Wednesday-SLA escalations are scheduled Tuesday to avoid depending on Wednesday's shortened day.

2. **Board numbers need early protection because the Thursday noon deadline depends on an uncertain external input and two uninterrupted hours.** Lina's Monday 15:00 vendor checkpoint provides the earliest warning. Tuesday 11:00–13:00 follows her stated corrections ETA, with Tuesday 14:00–16:00 reserved as a second full block. Finance gets the result early and must clarify finalization/approval requirements. Thursday morning is protected for final checks and contingency, but a last-minute two-hour reconciliation would consume every minute to the deadline; it is explicitly risky rather than promised as a safe fallback.

3. **The security patch needs early sequencing even though its deadline is Friday.** Review must precede a half-day of QA, which must precede deployment, and QA is unavailable Thursday. Using the 90-minute review estimate Monday gives QA a proposed Tuesday path and creates an opportunity to deploy Wednesday before the afternoon absence. This leaves recovery space before Friday 16:00. QA staffing, release windows and monitoring ownership remain unconfirmed, so the plan requests them and keeps a Friday fallback conditional on passing QA.

The strategy outline still receives a three-hour Monday focus block and an early send, using available time before the afternoon patch handoff. The customer demo moves to the offered Thursday alternative only once Product confirms it, with rehearsal inside Sam's known Wednesday availability. These choices preserve the hard deadlines, working-hour boundaries, daily lunch and meeting preference without inventing other people's availability. Buffers absorb modest overruns; larger dependency failures require named owners to agree scope, staffing or timing changes.
