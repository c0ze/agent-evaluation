# Threadmark — Launch Day Social

## LinkedIn post

Today we're launching Threadmark, a small desktop app for researchers buried in PDFs.

Point it at a folder of papers and notes, and it builds a local, searchable evidence map of what you actually have. The centerpiece is source-linked summaries: every sentence in a summary links back to the passage it came from. Click it and you're reading the original context, not taking the summary on faith.

Everything runs on your own computer. Your documents stay on your machine for processing — nothing is sent off to be read elsewhere.

Threadmark is $12/month after a 14-day trial, available now on Windows and macOS. We're a small team, so we're starting narrow: get the linking right, keep the app legible, and answer support ourselves.

If you live in folders of PDFs, try it and tell us where the map breaks down. Link in the comments.

## Replies

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Fair question. Threadmark isn't a chat window over your files — it's a map of them. It indexes a folder of PDFs and notes into a searchable evidence view, and the summaries exist so you can verify them: each sentence opens the supporting passage. Processing stays on your computer, and the 14-day trial is the honest test — see whether the links hold up on your own papers.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Right now Threadmark is sold as individual seats at $12/month after the 14-day trial, on Windows and macOS. We don't have a lab or site plan to offer today. If you tell us how many seats your lab needs and how you'd want billing to work, we'll read it carefully — that shapes what we build next. Reach us through the contact on our site.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Understood. Windows and macOS only at launch; Linux is something we're investigating but haven't promised. Logging this as one more vote for it.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. Local means processing happens on your computer and your documents stay there for that work. We don't train models on your papers.

### Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

That's not normal and I'm sorry — two hours stuck is a failed first run, not a slow one. Force-quit and relaunch, then try a smaller folder first to see if one file is tripping the indexer (scanned-image PDFs are the usual suspect). Then send us a support note with your OS and app version plus roughly how many files were in the folder, and we'll dig in. Please don't post any payment or account details here.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

It can, with one condition: your PDFs need selectable text rather than flat scans, in any language including Japanese. Threadmark maps and searches what's in the folder and links summaries back to the source passages, so a Japanese lit review works the same way. Try it on a small batch during the 14-day trial and check the links hold on your files.
