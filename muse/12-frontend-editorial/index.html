The River Keeps Its Receipts

By Imani Vale · Field essay · 24 min read

Along a drought-exposed riverbed, shop ledgers, bottle caps, and vanished street plans surface together. A field essay about civic memory and what infrastructure tries to forget.

Vale walks three kilometres of cracked bed with a retired lock-keeper, cataloguing what the water filed away: a 1962 bus token, a drowned arcade's prize shelf, the iron pins of a street that moved uphill. What emerges is not nostalgia but an audit — who kept records, who was never recorded, and what a city owes the ground it built on.

“Every drought is an audit. The river returns what we charged to it, with interest.”
