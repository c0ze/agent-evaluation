From the cover story

Imani Vale · p. 23

The river went down the way a shopkeeper closes: slowly, then all at once, leaving the day's takings in plain sight. Ledger pages stiffened in the sun. A street that the 1962 plan swore was never built reappeared as a seam of cobbles, exact to the metre.

“What a city forgets on purpose, a drought returns without ceremony.”

Vale walks four kilometres of exposed bed with a surveyor, a retired ferryman, and a box of water-warped receipts — and asks who gets to file the official memory.
