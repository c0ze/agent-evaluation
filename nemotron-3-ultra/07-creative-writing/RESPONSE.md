The story moves from quiet resignation—Mara performing the rituals of preservation in a town already written off—through a rupture of temporal impossible knowledge that initially feels like violation or haunting, toward the revelation that the "future message" is not a prediction but an invitation: the future is not fixed but forged by the choices she makes *now*. The emotional arc lands on agency reclaimed, not through grand salvation but through a single, specific act of reaching out (the dead phone, the text to her brother), transforming the archive from a mausoleum of goodbyes into a tool for return.
