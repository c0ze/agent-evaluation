# Moss — design response

## Personality

Moss is warm, tactile, and optimistic, with a little oddness kept on a short leash. The page feels like a potting bench rather than a dashboard: a dotted warm-sand ground, deep moss green ink, terracotta for anything that needs a drink, and a muted sun yellow reserved for small rewards. Each plant gets its own tint and its own corner treatment — cards alternate which corner is squared off, due badges sit at a slight tilt like stickers, and plant portraits live in blobby, hand-pressed shapes instead of identical circles. Serif display type (local Georgia/Palatino stack, no downloaded fonts) gives names like Frida, Orbit, Dot, and Drama a storybook weight, while plain sans body text keeps instructions easy to read. Delight is small and earned: a brief burst of leaves when a plant is watered, a striped progress bar that fills as the queue clears, and an all-done sun illustration. There are no neon gradients, glass panels, or walls of identical cards — urgency, not uniformity, drives the hierarchy, so overdue Frida is visibly larger and terracotta-bordered while tomorrow's Drama stays quiet.

## Interaction model

Today's care queue is sorted by urgency, so the one overdue task (Frida the fern, "Water — 2 days overdue") is always first and explicitly labelled "Start here." Every plant card offers the same two obvious actions: a primary **Watered/Done** button (worded "Watered" for water and mist tasks, "Done" for rotate/check tasks) and a **Snooze** button that rests the plant until tomorrow. Both actions remove the plant from the active queue, update the "cared for today" progress, and raise a notification with an **Undo** button that stays available for six seconds; snoozed plants also collect in a "Snoozed until tomorrow" list with their own "Bring back" control, so nothing ever silently disappears. Selecting **Details** on any card (or acting inside the panel) loads that plant into the detail panel, which shows light needs, water guidance, next task, current status, and a dated observation history, and offers the same water/snooze actions there. A compact weekly rhythm chart shows care visits per day (Mon 1, Tue 3, Wed 2, Thu 4, Fri 2, Sat 1, Sun 3) with today highlighted. When the queue empties, a useful all-done state replaces it: it confirms the day is clear, names the next upcoming care (Drama's mist tomorrow) or lists who is snoozed, and offers "Review snoozed plants" and "Bring everyone back" to reset. On desktop (900px and up) the layout becomes a true two-column composition — queue and greeting on the left, a sticky detail/week/notes sidebar on the right — rather than a stretched mobile column; on mobile everything stacks and the detail panel scrolls into view when selected.

## Accessibility choices

All controls are native `<button>` elements with descriptive `aria-label`s where the visible label alone would be ambiguous, so the full flow — water, snooze, undo, details, bring back, reset — works by keyboard alone, including Escape to dismiss the notification. Focus is always visible via a thick terracotta `:focus-visible` outline, and the notification moves focus to Undo so the undo path is reachable without hunting. Landmarks, a skip link to the queue, ordered headings, and a real list for the queue give screen-reader users the same priority order sighted users see. The toast region uses `aria-live="polite"`, the progress bar exposes `role="progressbar"` with value text, and the week chart is a `role="img"` graphic with a text equivalent list, so no information lives only in colour or bar height — overdue state is also written out as a text badge. Touch targets are at least 44px, body text keeps strong contrast against the warm ground, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` media query (plus a matching JavaScript check) disables the leaf burst, smooth scrolling, and transitions for people who ask for calm.
