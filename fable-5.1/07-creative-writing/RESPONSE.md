The arc moves Ines from professional detachment, cataloguing other people's unsaid things with a toothbrush and a form, into the private dread of being addressed by her own voice, which she first hears as a sentence: she will stay, the water will reach the step, she will become her father. The visit to Ferry Lane and the second listen quietly relocate the message to her mother and the year she left, so that "I kept the key" and "it was the same for me" turn from prophecy into an apology and a kind of permission, without the recording ever being explained away. The ending is meant to feel like acceptance rather than resolution: Ines files the message under the reason the living never send them, and chooses to let the future date stand.
