# Threadmark — launch day social

## 1. LinkedIn post

Threadmark is available today for Windows and macOS.

It is a small desktop app for people who work from folders of research PDFs and notes. Point it at a folder and it builds a local, searchable evidence map of what is inside.

The part we spent the longest on is source-linked summaries. Every sentence Threadmark writes is tied to the passage it came from. Click the sentence and the original page opens beside it. A summary should be a set of pointers you can check, not a paragraph you have to take on faith.

Processing stays on your computer. Your folder is not sent anywhere to be indexed.

It costs $12 a month after a 14-day trial. Linux is something we are looking into, though we are not promising it yet.

If your weeks involve tracing which paper actually said what, we hope you will give it a try.

## 4. Replies to sample comments

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Fair to ask, since there are a lot of those. Threadmark is not a chat window over your files. It builds a searchable map of the folder, and the summaries it produces are source-linked: click any sentence and the supporting passage opens beside it, so you can check the claim instead of trusting it. Processing stays on your computer. If you try the trial and it still feels like a chatbot in a trench coat, I would like to know what was missing.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Right now it is individual only: $12 a month per person after the 14-day trial. We have not built lab or institutional purchasing, and I do not want to imply that it exists. If you can share roughly how many seats you would need and how your university normally buys software, I will tell you plainly whether we can make it work.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Understood. Windows and macOS today. Linux is something we're looking at, but we haven't promised it because we don't want to sell you a date we can't keep. If that changes, we'll say so here.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. Processing happens on your computer, so your PDFs aren't uploaded to us, and we can't train on what we don't have. "Local" here means the folder stays where it is.

### Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

That's not how your first day with it should go, and I'm sorry. Two hours with no movement isn't something to wait out. Please send us a DM with your operating system, roughly how many PDFs are in the folder, and whether the progress count has changed at all. Keep your email and payment details out of the comments; the DM is the right place for those. We'll work through it with you from there.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Honest answer: I don't want to promise it until you've seen it on your own sources. The 14-day trial is free, so drop a few of your Japanese PDFs into a folder and check whether the source links land on the right passages. If they don't, tell us. That's exactly the kind of gap we want to hear about.
