# Why these three come first

The inbox has five competing asks and roughly 31 working hours after meetings
and lunch (Wednesday afternoon is gone). Only four items carry a hard deadline
someone else stated: the Monday renewals escalation, the Wednesday SLAs, board
numbers by Thursday 12:00, and the patch by Friday 16:00. Everything else — the
CEO's "early this week", Product's preferred demo slot — is a preference with a
stated alternative. So the top three are the hard-deadline chains, ordered by
when their irreversible step has to happen and how fragile the chain is, not by
who asked.

## 1. Start the security patch chain on Monday

The patch has the longest dependency chain: my review (60–90 min) → a QA half
day → a 30-minute change window, deployed by Friday 16:00, with QA unavailable
Thursday. If the review happens Monday, QA can go Tuesday, the deploy can sit
at Friday 10:00, and there is room for exactly one fix-and-re-test loop
(fix Wed, re-review Thu, re-test Fri morning, deploy Fri 14:00). If the review
slips to Tuesday, that loop disappears; slip to Wednesday and Friday morning is
the only QA day left, with the deploy at 14:00 and no margin at all. The work
itself is small, which is exactly why it goes first: 90 minutes on Monday buys
the most contingency of anything on the list.

## 2. Protect the board-numbers block and manage the Lina dependency

This is the highest-stakes item — a fixed board cutoff at Thursday 12:00 — but
its critical action cannot start before Lina's corrections land, so its place
in the plan is about protection rather than sequencing. The two uninterrupted
hours are booked Tuesday 11:00–13:00 with nothing adjacent, and the only two
remaining two-hour slots (Tuesday 14:00–16:00, Thursday 10:00–12:00) are held
in reserve. Wednesday offers no two-hour slot at all, so the real risk is
Lina's vendor export. That's why her Monday 15:00 status is treated as a
checkpoint, why Finance is asked now what "final" means and whether a
preliminary set helps, and why the plan refuses to put board work on Wednesday.

## 3. Clear the support escalations early — the renewals one first

The renewals escalation is due Monday, touches revenue, and takes 45 minutes,
so it is the first piece of real work on Monday, before anything that could
overrun. The two Wednesday-SLA escalations are done Monday afternoon and
Tuesday, not Wednesday, because Wednesday morning is already the only
rehearsal slot Sam can make and Wednesday afternoon is unavailable. Leaving
them for Wednesday would turn a comfortable deadline into a genuine one.

## Why the CEO's outline is fourth, not first

It's the most senior ask and it still gets three focused hours on Monday
afternoon, because that is the only place three hours fit before Tuesday
14:00. But the 14:00 is an opportunity to be reviewed at leadership, not a
deadline the CEO set, and the CEO has an obvious fallback (async review
Wednesday or the next meeting). If Monday's hard items overrun, the outline is
what gives — and the draft reply says so rather than promising the slot.

## Why the demo is fifth

Product's preferred slot is inside my unavailable block, and the customer has
already offered Thursday 16:00. The rehearsal is fixed by Sam's availability
and is booked; the demo itself is a decision for Product and the customer, so
the plan proposes Thursday and waits rather than inventing availability for
Sam, the customer, or me.
