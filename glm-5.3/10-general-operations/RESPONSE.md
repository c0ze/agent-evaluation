# Response — reasoning behind the top three priorities

## Priority 1: Renewals escalation (Monday, first thing after the recurring meeting)

This is the only item with a same-day hard deadline and direct revenue
impact. "Needs a response Monday" leaves zero scheduling freedom, so it goes
first — before anything negotiable. Everything else in the week has at least
one fallback slot; this one has none. It's also small (45 minutes), so doing it
first costs almost nothing and removes the only item that could fail on day
one.

## Priority 2: Finance board numbers (2h block Tuesday 11:00–13:00, final Thursday 12:00)

The deadline is hard and external (the board), and the work is gated on
someone else: Lina's corrections, due Tuesday by 11:00 but conditional on a
vendor export. That makes it the week's biggest coordination risk. I placed
the two uninterrupted hours immediately after the corrections are due (Tue
11:00–13:00) so that if things slip, I still have Tuesday afternoon,
Wednesday morning, and Thursday before 12:00 as fallbacks — three escape hatches
before an immovable deadline. I also set a checkpoint at Lina's Monday 15:00
signal so a late vendor export is known a full day early rather than
discovered at the deadline.

## Priority 3: Security patch review (Tuesday 09:30–11:00, deploy by Friday 16:00)

This is a hard deadline wrapped around the most fragile dependency chain of
the week: my review (60–90 min) → QA (half a day, and QA is unavailable
Thursday) → a 30-minute change window. Any slip cascades. I chose Tuesday
morning for the review because it's the latest slot that still allows QA on
Wednesday, the change window on Thursday, and keeps *all of Friday* as
contingency before the 16:00 deadline. Reviewing later than Tuesday morning
means QA lands on Friday, leaving essentially no room for a failed QA pass.

## What I deliberately ranked below these

- **Customer demo**: important, but it has a workable alternative (Thursday
  16:00), and its blocker is a decision, not my time — so it's priority in
  DECISIONS.md, not in my calendar.
- **Strategy outline**: the CEO's "early this week" is a preference, not a
  hard date; drafting Monday and sending EOD beats the Tuesday 14:00 cutoff
  with margin. It got prime Monday focus time because three focused hours
  only exist if I protect them, but it yields to the three priorities above.

## How hard constraints shaped the plan

I treated working hours, Wednesday 13:00–17:30 unavailability, all stated
deadlines, and QA's Thursday unavailability as immovable. I treated the
Wednesday 15:00 demo preference, the CEO's timing, the meeting cap, and lunch
as preferences to honor — and did — but never at the cost of a hard deadline.
Where a preference and a hard constraint collide (the demo), I surfaced the
conflict instead of quietly assuming I could attend.
