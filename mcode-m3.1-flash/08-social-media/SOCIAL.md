# Threadmark — launch day

## 1. LinkedIn post

Threadmark is a small desktop app that turns folders of research PDFs and notes into a local, searchable evidence map.

Every summary sentence is source-linked. Click it and the supporting passage opens right next to it — so you can check a claim against the paper instead of trusting a paragraph that sounds confident.

Processing happens on your computer. Your documents get indexed where they already live, not uploaded to a service that has to promise it won't read them. For work that hasn't been published yet, that seems like the minimum.

The launch feature is source-linked summaries. It's $12/month after a 14-day trial. Windows and macOS are available now; Linux isn't yet — we're looking into it, and we'd rather tell you that plainly than put a date on it.

If you've ever highlighted something in a PDF and lost the highlight, this is for you.

## 4. Replies

### 4.1 LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Partly, and it's fair to ask. There is a model doing the summarizing, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What it can't do is leave a summary unattached to its own evidence — every sentence carries a pointer to the passage behind it, so a line that overreaches is one click from being caught.

The other difference is architectural. The model runs on your machine against files already sitting in your folder, rather than your corpus being uploaded somewhere to become product.

The honest way to test this is on a paper you've already read closely. You'd spot a wrong summary in about a minute, which is more than a marketing page can prove.

### 4.2 LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Not today, and I'd rather be straight about that than leave you guessing. It's $12/month per person after the 14-day trial, paid individually — there's no institutional invoicing or seat-billing layer yet.

If a lab actually needs one invoice, or a security review, that's a real blocker rather than a paperwork detail. Send us a note with a rough headcount and what specifically is stopping a purchase, and we'll tell you whether it's possible now or plainly not. Requests like that are what a licensing tier would get built from, but nothing like it is committed today.

### 4.3 Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Fair. It isn't there, and a waitlist signup isn't a release date.

Windows and macOS are live now. If Linux is the blocker, say what you're running and what you need from it — that list is the real input to whether it happens.

### 4.4 Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. Your papers aren't training data.

"Local" means the model is already on your machine and your files are read and summarized there, not uploaded somewhere to learn from. Ask me what gets written to disk and I'll point you at the actual behaviour.

### 4.5 Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

That sounds genuinely stuck rather than slow, and I'm sorry you're paying while it sits there. Two things worth trying before anything else: quit and reopen the app, since indexing usually resumes from where it stopped; and if it hangs on the same folder again, drop a single PDF into an empty folder and index that, which tells you whether it's the file or the folder size. I can't tell which from here.

For the real fix, please use the support button inside the app rather than posting here — it attaches your logs and version automatically, and those logs are the only honest way we'll find what stalled. Your account details don't belong in a public thread.

### 4.6 Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Untested, so I'd rather say that than promise it. We test primarily in English, and we haven't run a Japanese literature review end to end.

Worth trying though: if your PDFs have selectable text, the language is largely a matter of the model handling it, and the linking workflow is the same. If they're scans, you'll need OCR first. Tell us how it goes either way — that's how the supported-language list actually gets decided.
