# Launch day social posts — Threadmark

## LinkedIn post

Today we launched Threadmark, a small desktop app that turns folders of research PDFs and notes into a local, searchable evidence map.

The launch feature is source-linked summaries. Every sentence in a summary links back to the passage it came from — click it, and the supporting text opens right there. No more digging to page 47 to check whether a claim is real.

Everything is processed on your computer. Your papers and notes stay with you.

Threadmark is $12/month after a 14-day trial, available now for Windows and macOS. We're investigating Linux, but we aren't promising it yet.

We built this for people who read carefully — researchers, analysts, students, anyone who has ever looked at a sentence and asked, "where did that come from?" If that's you, the trial is there, and we'd genuinely like to hear what holds up and what doesn't.

## Replies to sample comments

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Fair question — the category is crowded. Threadmark isn't a chatbot: it doesn't generate free-form answers. It builds an evidence map of your PDFs and notes, and every sentence in a summary links to the exact passage it came from, so you can verify it in one click. If a sentence can't be traced back to your sources, that's a bug, not a feature. Happy to answer specifics.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

Thanks for asking. Right now Threadmark is sold as individual subscriptions, so each lab member would start their own trial and plan. If that doesn't fit how your lab buys software, email us and we'll talk it through — we'd rather find a workable arrangement than have you force-fit a lab into per-seat checkout.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Understood. Linux is being investigated, not promised — we'd rather say that plainly than string you along. If it ships, we'll post about it here.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. Local means local: processing happens on your computer, and we don't train on your papers or notes. Your library stays yours.

### Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

Sorry — two hours stuck is not the experience we want anyone paying for. First step: quit and reopen the app; indexing resumes where it left off, so you won't lose progress. If it's still stuck after that, use Help → Send Diagnostics and message us here so we can dig in. Please keep your account and library details to DMs or the diagnostics file rather than posting them publicly. We'll get it sorted.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

Honest answer: we haven't validated Threadmark thoroughly on Japanese text yet, so we don't want to overpromise. Indexing and source-linking depend on how the text extracts from your PDFs, which varies with the files themselves. The fairest way to find out is the free 14-day trial with a handful of your actual papers — and if you do, we'd love to hear what works and what breaks.
