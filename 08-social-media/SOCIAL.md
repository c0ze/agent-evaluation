# SOCIAL.md — Threadmark launch day

## 1. LinkedIn post (157 words)

Today we're releasing Threadmark, a small desktop app for people who read a lot of PDFs.

If your research folder has quietly become a second memory — three hundred papers, eleven of them bookmarked, one you can no longer find — you know the problem. Search finds the file. It doesn't find the claim.

Threadmark builds a searchable evidence map from your folders of PDFs and notes. The launch feature is source-linked summaries: click any sentence in a summary and the supporting passage opens beside it. Nothing leaps to a chatbot answer you then have to verify; every claim keeps its source attached.

Processing happens on your own computer. Your papers don't travel anywhere to be summarized.

Windows and macOS, $12/month, with a 14-day trial so you can run it against your real folder before you pay for it.

We're a small team. If it chokes on your folder, we want to hear about that more than anything else.

## 4. Replies to sample comments

**LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"**

No chatbot box in the launch version. You get summaries written across your own files, where every sentence is a link to the passage it came from, so checking the source is one click. The evidence map sits underneath it so you can see what you already have before you ask anything of it. If a summary and its source disagree, the source is the one that opens.

**LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"**

Not at the moment. Today a subscription is tied to one person's account, $12/month after a 14-day trial, and there's no site licence or seat pool to offer you. That's genuinely awkward for a shared lab machine, and I'd rather say so than imply otherwise is coming. If you tell me roughly how many people you're equipping, I'll tell you honestly where we are.

**Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"**

Fair. Linux isn't shipping today. Windows and macOS are what we can install, test and break ourselves on right now. A Linux build is being investigated, but it isn't promised and I won't give you a date. If your work is Linux-only, the trial won't help you.

**Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"**

No training on your documents. Local means the summaries are built on your machine, not on a server copy of your files, and nothing about your papers goes into training. Don't take our word: run the trial and watch the network, then tell us what you find.

**Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."**

Sorry. A frozen progress bar for two hours is not worth $12. Please don't post order or account details here. DM us your OS (Windows or macOS), the version in the app's About box, and roughly how many files the folder held, and we'll treat it as a stuck-index bug until we've shown it isn't. If you'd rather have a refund, the same DM is the place.

**Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"**

We don't know yet, and I'd rather say so than guess. Text extraction on Japanese isn't something we've tested, so we can't tell you it works. The trial is free and your files stay on your machine, so point it at one folder and see whether the passages it finds are the ones you'd find. If it goes wrong, tell us what happened.
