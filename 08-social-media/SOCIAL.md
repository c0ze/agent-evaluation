## Replies

### LinkedIn — "Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?"

Fair question. Threadmark doesn't chat with your documents. It builds a searchable map and writes summaries where each sentence links to the passage it came from — you click a line and see the source in your own file. Processing runs locally, so nothing is sent off your machine. It's closer to a very careful index than a chatbot.

### LinkedIn — "Can a university buy seats for a lab?"

We don't have a team or institutional license today — Threadmark is an individual app at $12/month per user after the trial. If you're equipping a lab, each person can start their own trial and subscribe. Multi-seat licensing isn't something we're offering right now, and I don't want to promise a route that doesn't exist yet.

### Bluesky — "no linux, no deal"

Understood — Linux is the one gap. We're looking into it but haven't committed to a date, so I won't pretend it's imminent. Windows and macOS are what work today. If Linux is a hard requirement, Threadmark isn't there for you yet.

### Bluesky — "Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?"

No. "Local" means the processing happens on your computer and your PDFs and notes aren't uploaded. We don't train models on your work — there's no server-side model doing anything with your files. Your documents stay in the folder you put them in.

### Instagram — "I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours."

Two hours isn't right, and I'm sorry — that's not what should happen after you've paid. One useful first step: indexing can stall on a single odd PDF, so try quitting and reopening the app, then add your folder in smaller batches to see which file it stops on. Please send us a direct message with what you're seeing so we can look at your case properly — I'd rather not ask for account details in a public thread.

### Instagram — "Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?"

The source-link part will work: each summary sentence links back to the original passage, so you can check it against the Japanese text yourself. I'd be cautious about the summaries, though — I can't promise their quality for Japanese-language PDFs, since we haven't validated that. For a review where accuracy matters, I'd treat the evidence map as a way to navigate your sources, not a substitute for reading them.
