# Three riskiest assumptions

1. **Supervisors can wait for every tablet copy to be released before acceptance.** A powered-off tablet cannot act on an acceptance notification within 24 hours. The plan keeps acceptance pending until each registered copy has verified removal evidence. Walk operations through a lost tablet and an urgent acceptance, then race a new download against acceptance with two tablets. If the delay is unacceptable or any download can bypass registration, hold pilot enrollment until an alternative can meet the deadline.
2. **A managed tablet can preserve a full visit through failure and erase all of its customer data on release.** Forty representative photos, temporary files and a signature may exhaust storage. Old SQLite journals, thumbnails or backups could retain a second copy. On the least-capable pilot tablet, interrupt saves and uploads under storage pressure, recover every item shown as saved, then inspect all storage and backup paths after release. Qualify only configurations that pass and obtain the regulatory owner's approval of the removal evidence.
3. **All writers expose enough version history to resolve edits and preserve what the inspector signed.** A supervisor changing the same answer after offline signing could silently alter the submitted record or force a return visit. Exercise the actual web and tablet write paths with independent edits, competing edits to one note, a photo replacement and a lost submission response. Require a traceable resolution and a new signature whenever the reviewed content changes. Confirm that field operations can obtain it before expanding the pilot.
