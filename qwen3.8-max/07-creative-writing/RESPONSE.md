The story is meant to travel from numb custodianship through dread and grief to release. June first hears the future message as evidence of a stranded, wasted life, and the dread tightens around the one recording she refuses to open: her mother's. Once she finally listens, the identical sentences return as permission rather than warning, turning dread into companionship across time. The final gesture — sending instead of keeping — should leave the reader with hard-won continuity rather than tidy resolution.
