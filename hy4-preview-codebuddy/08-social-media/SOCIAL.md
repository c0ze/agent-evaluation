# Threadmark — launch day social

## 1. LinkedIn (120–180 words)

Threadmark is live today.

It's a small desktop app for people who keep a folder of research PDFs and a worse
folder of notes about them. Point it at a folder and it builds a local, searchable
evidence map: every summary sentence is linked to the passage it came from, so
clicking a line takes you back to the source instead of asking you to trust it.

That link is the whole point. A summary you can't check is only a claim; a summary
that opens the page it came from is a citation you can follow.

Everything is processed on your own computer. Your documents don't leave it, and
they aren't used to train anything.

Threadmark is $12/month after a 14-day free trial. Windows and macOS are available
now.

We're a small team, and the app is deliberately narrow: one job, on your machine,
with receipts. If your reading has outgrown your folder structure, it might be
worth fourteen days.

## 4. Replies to the six sample comments

**LinkedIn — “Is this just another chatbot wrapping my documents?”**

Fair question, and the answer is no. There's no chat box to talk to. You point
Threadmark at a folder and it builds a searchable evidence map where each summary
sentence is linked to the passage it came from — the output is a browseable map
with citations, not a conversation. Processing runs on your own computer, so
nothing is uploaded. If you want the short test: give it a folder you already
know well during the trial. If the links point where you'd point them, it's doing
its job; if they don't, it isn't, and that's visible in about two minutes.

**LinkedIn — “Can a university buy seats for a lab?”**

Not yet, and I'd rather say that plainly than imply otherwise. Today Threadmark is
a single-user subscription at $12/month after a 14-day trial — there's no
institutional billing, no shared admin console, no seat management. If you DM me
the lab size and how you'd want accounts handled, I'll tell you exactly what
works today and what we'd have to build. I'm not going to sell you a plan that
doesn't exist.

**Bluesky — “no linux, no deal”**

Completely fair. Windows and macOS only right now. Linux is something we're
looking into, not something we've built or dated, so I won't sell it to you. If
that changes, it'll be a real release, not a hint.

**Bluesky — “Does local mean you train on my papers anyway?”**

No. Processing happens on your machine; the files aren't uploaded and aren't used
for training. Local here is the architecture, not a toggle — there's no server
call holding your documents that we could quietly keep a copy of.

**Instagram — “I paid and the app has been stuck indexing for two hours.”**

That isn't how it should behave, and I'm sorry it's where your trial went. Two
hours stuck on one folder is something we want to look at rather than talk
around. Send us a DM with your operating system, the app version, and roughly how
many files are in that folder — please don't send account details or any of the
documents themselves. We'll take it from there with you directly.

**Instagram — “Would this work for a literature review in Japanese?”**

Honest answer: we haven't tested it with Japanese sources, so I won't promise
it. What I can tell you is how it works — it reads the text layer of your PDFs
and links each summary sentence to the passage behind it, so results depend on
how cleanly that text comes out of your files. The useful next step is to run one
small Japanese folder through the 14-day trial before paying. If the links land
where you'd expect, you'll know quickly.
